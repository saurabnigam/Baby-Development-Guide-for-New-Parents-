# EQ & IQ Skills Tracker

> Companion to [Module 21: Building EQ](../21-building-eq/README.md) and [Module 22: Building IQ](../22-building-iq/README.md).

---

## How to use this

**Track quarterly, not daily.** These capacities move over seasons. Tracking them weekly turns development into a performance and puts your child in the position of being assessed by you — which is exactly the dynamic that suppresses the behaviours you're trying to grow.

**Four levels for every skill:**

| Level | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **—** | Not yet — and that may be completely age-appropriate |
| **W** | With help — happens when an adult supports or prompts it |
| **S** | Sometimes — happens alone, inconsistently |
| **O** | On their own — including when nobody is watching |

**The column that matters is O.** A child who does it when watched has learned a rule. A child who does it unwatched has learned a skill.

**Date every review.** The value of this document is the comparison between two dates, not any single snapshot.

## A caution about this document

This is a tool for *you*, to notice growth you would otherwise miss and to choose where to put your effort. It is not an assessment, and it should never be shown to your child as a scorecard or discussed as a list of what they can't do yet.

The blanks are not deficits. Most of them are just "not yet," and "not yet" is the normal state of a developing human for eighteen years.

If several rows in a section are blank well past the typical window, or if you see skills *disappear* between reviews, that's worth a conversation with your paediatrician — bring this document with you, because organised observation over time is genuinely useful clinical information.
