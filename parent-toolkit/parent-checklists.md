# Parent Checklists

> Use these checklists to turn the guide into repeatable habits.

---

## Daily Checklist: 0 to 12 Months

- I made time for face-to-face interaction.
- I responded to my baby’s sounds, expressions, or gestures.
- I used talking, singing, or reading during care routines.
- My baby had safe movement time, such as tummy time or floor time.
- I watched for hunger, fatigue, and overstimulation cues.
- Screens did not replace direct interaction.
- I noticed one thing my baby is learning right now.

---

## Daily Checklist: 3 to 6 Months

Use this when your baby is moving out of the newborn stage and into more active social, hand, mouth, and movement exploration.

- My baby was fed on hunger cues and not pressured past fullness.
- I followed the vitamin D, iron, or medicine plan discussed with the pediatrician.
- My baby had several short chances for safe floor time or tummy time.
- I placed one safe toy within reach and waited before helping.
- I responded to at least a few sounds, smiles, looks, or movements.
- I used one repeated song, book, phrase, or mirror game.
- My baby explored one safe object with hands or mouth while supervised.
- I watched for tired signs, overstimulation, and the need for a break.
- Sleep happened on a firm, flat, empty surface on the back.
- Screens did not replace face, voice, touch, movement, or real play.

### Weekly Feeding and Health Check: 3 to 6 Months

- Milk is still the main food: breast milk or iron-fortified formula.
- No water, juice, honey, cow's milk as a drink, salt, sugar, or solids before readiness.
- I know whether my baby needs vitamin D drops.
- I have asked, or will ask, about iron at the 4-month checkup.
- I am watching wet diapers, growth, and feeding comfort.
- I am preparing for solids around 6 months using readiness signs, not pressure.
- I have a local pediatrician plan for well visits and vaccines.

---

## Daily Checklist: 1 to 2 Years

- I offered simple choices instead of unnecessary power struggles.
- My child had active movement time.
- We had at least one calm language-rich routine together.
- I named feelings at least once during the day.
- I let my child try something independently before stepping in.
- Meals and snacks were reasonably predictable.
- I helped with regulation instead of expecting instant self-control.

---

## Daily Checklist: 2 to 5 Years

- My child had open-ended play time.
- I used real conversation, not only instructions.
- I asked at least one thinking question and one feeling question.
- My child had physical movement and outdoor time if possible.
- I kept at least one routine predictable.
- I coached a small social or emotional skill.
- Screens did not crowd out play, sleep, or connection.

---

## Weekly Checklist: Home Environment

- The home setup supports safe exploration.
- Toys and materials are simple enough for focused play.
- Mealtimes are mostly calm and not driven by conflict.
- Sleep routines are predictable enough to support regulation.
- Adults are getting enough support to stay reasonably regulated.
- There is some margin in the schedule for rest and free play.

---

## Weekly Checklist: Development Review

- I noticed at least one new skill or behavior.
- I saw what currently frustrates my child most.
- I saw what currently motivates my child most.
- I noticed whether sleep, food, or transitions are affecting behavior.
- I adjusted one expectation to better fit my child’s stage.

---

## Weekly Checklist: Parent Self-Check

- I am not using social media comparison as my main benchmark.
- I am asking whether behavior is developmental before taking it personally.
- I am protecting at least one daily connection ritual.
- I am asking for support when I need it.
- I am remembering that repair matters more than perfection.

---

## Red-Flag Review Checklist

Use this if you are deciding whether to seek professional input.

- There is a clear loss of skills.
- Concern is persistent, not just occasional.
- The issue appears across different settings or routines.
- Feeding, sleep, language, movement, or social engagement is being affected significantly.
- My concern keeps returning even when I try reasonable support strategies.
- My intuition says this needs a closer look.

If several of these are true, document what you are seeing and speak with your pediatrician or another qualified professional.

---

## Monthly Reset Checklist

- Which routines are helping right now?
- Which recurring struggles need better structure?
- Which module should I revisit this month?
- What one habit would make the biggest difference in our home?

Pick one change only. Do not redesign the whole family system at once.
