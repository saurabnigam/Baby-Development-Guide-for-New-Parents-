# Module 02: Developmental Milestones Overview — IQ & EQ from Birth to 18

> **Time**: 2 hours | **Difficulty**: Beginner | **For**: All parents and caregivers

---

## Key Takeaways

- Development follows a predictable sequence but at each child's own pace
- Cognitive (IQ) and emotional (EQ) milestones are deeply interconnected
- The 0–5 period contains the most milestones and the fastest growth
- Milestones are screening guideposts — missing one is not a diagnosis
- Tracking milestones helps you provide the right stimulation at the right time

---

## Developmental Theories That Guide This Course

### Jean Piaget — Cognitive Development Stages

| Stage | Age | Key Features |
|-------|-----|-------------|
| **Sensorimotor** | 0–2 years | Learns through senses and movement; develops object permanence |
| **Preoperational** | 2–7 years | Symbolic thinking, language explosion, egocentrism, imaginative play |
| **Concrete Operational** | 7–11 years | Logical thinking about concrete events, conservation, classification |
| **Formal Operational** | 12+ years | Abstract thinking, hypothetical reasoning, metacognition |

### Erik Erikson — Psychosocial Stages

| Stage | Age | Central Question | Healthy Outcome |
|-------|-----|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Trust vs Mistrust** | 0–18 months | "Can I trust the world?" | Hope and security |
| **Autonomy vs Shame** | 18 months–3 years | "Can I do things myself?" | Will and independence |
| **Initiative vs Guilt** | 3–5 years | "Is it okay to explore and act?" | Purpose and direction |
| **Industry vs Inferiority** | 5–12 years | "Can I succeed at tasks?" | Competence and confidence |
| **Identity vs Role Confusion** | 12–18 years | "Who am I?" | Fidelity and self-identity |

### John Bowlby & Mary Ainsworth — Attachment Theory

| Attachment Style | Caregiver Pattern | Child Outcome |
|-----------------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Secure** (65%) | Responsive, consistent, warm | Confident exploration, healthy relationships |
| **Anxious-Resistant** (10%) | Inconsistent responsiveness | Clingy, anxious, difficulty with separation |
| **Avoidant** (20%) | Emotionally distant, dismissive | Emotionally shut down, pseudo-independence |
| **Disorganized** (5%) | Frightening or frightened behavior | Confused, contradictory behavior |

*Secure attachment is the single strongest predictor of positive developmental outcomes across IQ and EQ domains (Sroufe et al., 2005).*

## The Complete Milestone Map

### 0–3 Months: Sensory Awakening

| Domain | Milestones |
|--------|-----------|
| **Cognitive (IQ)** | Recognizes faces at close range; tracks moving objects; prefers patterns and contrasts; startles to loud sounds; begins distinguishing mother's voice |
| **Emotional (EQ)** | Social smile (6–8 weeks); calms when held; begins self-soothing (hand-to-mouth); shows distress through crying; recognizes primary caregiver |
| **Motor** | Lifts head briefly during tummy time; reflexive grasping; brings hands to midline |
| **Language** | Coos and gurgles; differentiates between speech sounds; quiets to familiar voice |

### 3–6 Months: Social Engagement

| Domain | Milestones |
|--------|-----------|
| **Cognitive (IQ)** | Reaches for objects; visually follows objects 180°; explores objects with mouth; begins understanding cause-effect (shaking a rattle) |
| **Emotional (EQ)** | Laughs; distinguishes familiar people from strangers; expresses joy, sadness, anger; responds to others' emotions; enjoys social play |
| **Motor** | Rolls over; sits with support; reaches and grasps; transfers objects between hands |
| **Language** | Babbles (consonant-vowel combos like "ba", "ma"); responds to own name; turns toward sounds |

### 6–9 Months: Exploration & Object Permanence

| Domain | Milestones |
|--------|-----------|
| **Cognitive (IQ)** | Object permanence emerging (searches for hidden objects); understands "no"; explores objects more systematically; imitates simple actions |
| **Emotional (EQ)** | Stranger anxiety appears; separation anxiety begins; shows preference for specific people; uses social referencing (looks to parent to gauge safety) |
| **Motor** | Sits independently; begins crawling; picks up small objects (pincer grasp developing) |
| **Language** | Babbling becomes more varied; may say "mama" or "dada" (not yet specific); understands some words; uses gestures (reaching) |

### 9–12 Months: Language & Attachment

| Domain | Milestones |
|--------|-----------|
| **Cognitive (IQ)** | Finds hidden objects; uses objects as tools; understands simple cause-effect chains; explores containers (in and out); points to indicate interest |
| **Emotional (EQ)** | Clear attachment behavior (seeks specific caregiver when distressed); separation anxiety peaks; shows wide range of emotions; begins understanding others' intentions |
| **Motor** | Pulls to standing; cruises along furniture; may take first steps; precise pincer grasp |
| **Language** | First true words; understands ~50 words; follows simple instructions ("give me the ball"); uses pointing and gestures to communicate |

### 12–18 Months: Walking & Language Explosion

| Domain | Milestones |
|--------|-----------|
| **Cognitive (IQ)** | Walks independently (expand exploration); solves simple problems (pulling a blanket to get a toy); recognizes self in mirror; begins symbolic play (pretend drinking from cup) |
| **Emotional (EQ)** | Uses caregiver as "secure base" for exploration; shows jealousy; begins emerging empathy; expresses frustration, pride; separation anxiety may intensify then gradually decrease |
| **Motor** | Walking (9–17 months); begins climbing; stacks 2–3 blocks; scribbles with crayon |
| **Language** | Vocabulary explosion (6+ new words/week); points to named objects; may combine gestures and words; 10–50 word vocabulary |

### 18–24 Months: Emotional Regulation Begins

| Domain | Milestones |
|--------|-----------|
| **Cognitive (IQ)** | Sorts shapes and colors; engages in make-believe play; completes simple puzzles; understands "mine" vs "yours"; follows 2-step instructions |
| **Emotional (EQ)** | "The terrible twos" begin — not terrible, but intense; tantrums emerge as emotional regulation is still developing; emerging self-consciousness (embarrassment, pride); begins parallel play |
| **Motor** | Runs; kicks a ball; walks up stairs with help; uses spoon/fork; builds towers of 4–6 blocks |
| **Language** | 50–200+ words; two-word phrases ("more milk", "daddy go"); simple questions; names familiar objects |

### 2–3 Years: Self-Identity & Theory of Mind

| Domain | Milestones |
|--------|-----------|
| **Cognitive (IQ)** | Names objects; counts to 3–5; understands "big/small", "in/out"; matches shapes; beginning of categorization; memory improves significantly |
| **Emotional (EQ)** | Uses "I", "me", "mine"; begins identifying emotions in self and others; parallel play transitions toward interactive play; developing empathy; can be comforted by words |
| **Motor** | Pedals tricycle; draws circles; uses scissors; dresses self partially; improved balance |
| **Language** | 200–1000+ words; 3–4 word sentences; asks "why" constantly; tells simple stories; understood by familiar adults 75% of the time |

### 3–4 Years: Imagination & Cooperation

| Domain | Milestones |
|--------|-----------|
| **Cognitive (IQ)** | Understands time concepts (today, tomorrow); counts to 10; names colors; draws simple figures; tells longer stories; beginning of logical reasoning |
| **Emotional (EQ)** | Cooperative play emerges; takes turns (with reminders); emerging theory of mind (understands others have different thoughts); negotiates with peers; imaginary friends common |
| **Motor** | Hops on one foot; catches bounced ball; draws person with 2–4 body parts; buttons large buttons |
| **Language** | 1,000–2,000 words; complex sentences; tells stories; asks many questions; rhyming and word play; understood by strangers 75%+ |

### 4–5 Years: Pre-Academic Skills & Empathy

| Domain | Milestones |
|--------|-----------|
| **Cognitive (IQ)** | Counts to 20+; recognizes some letters; understands sequencing; simple addition/subtraction with manipulatives; extended attention span (15+ minutes) |
| **Emotional (EQ)** | Theory of mind solidifying; understands others can have different beliefs; manages some emotions independently; developing conscience; more stable friendships |
| **Motor** | Skips; writes some letters; ties shoes (later in range); draws recognizable pictures; dominant hand established |
| **Language** | 2,000–5,000 words; adult-like grammar; tells detailed stories; understands jokes; can explain thoughts and feelings |

### 5–7 Years: School Readiness

| Domain | Milestones |
|--------|-----------|
| **Cognitive (IQ)** | Reads simple books; basic math operations; understands rules; increasing logical thinking; begins understanding conservation (Piaget) |
| **Emotional (EQ)** | Develops real friendships; understands and follows rules; beginnings of moral reasoning; increasing self-regulation; can delay gratification |

### 7–12 Years: Concrete Operations

| Domain | Milestones |
|--------|-----------|
| **Cognitive (IQ)** | Logical reasoning about concrete events; classification and seriation; conservation mastered; metacognition developing; academic skills deepen |
| **Emotional (EQ)** | Complex social navigation; peer influence increases; self-concept develops; understands nuanced emotions (jealousy, guilt, pride); increased independence |

### 12–18 Years: Formal Operations & Identity

| Domain | Milestones |
|--------|-----------|
| **Cognitive (IQ)** | Abstract thinking; hypothetical reasoning; systematic problem-solving; future orientation; philosophical thinking; prefrontal cortex still developing |
| **Emotional (EQ)** | Identity formation; intimate friendships; romantic relationships; values clarification; increased emotional intensity; developing emotional wisdom |

## Milestone Tracking: A Balanced Approach

### DO:
- Note when your child reaches milestones (a simple journal works fine)
- Share observations with your pediatrician at well-child visits
- Use milestones to guide what activities to introduce
- Celebrate each achievement

### DON'T:
- Compare your child to specific other children
- Panic if one milestone is slightly delayed
- Force skills before the child is developmentally ready
- Use milestone charts as the sole measure of your child's development

### When to Raise Concerns

**Consult your pediatrician if you notice:**
- No social smile by 3 months
- No babbling by 9 months
- No words by 16 months
- No two-word phrases by 24 months
- Loss of any previously acquired skill at any age
- No pointing or gesturing by 12 months
- No response to name by 12 months

---

## The Golden Ratio: 70/30 Rule

For optimal development at any stage, aim for approximately:
- **70% child-led** — Follow your child's interests, let them explore, join their play
- **30% adult-guided** — Introduce new concepts, scaffold challenges, structured activities

This ratio respects the child's natural curiosity while ensuring they're gently stretched beyond current abilities (Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development).

---

## 5-Minute Action Plan

**Create a simple milestone journal.** Get any notebook and write your child's current age at the top. Then write down 3 things they can do now that they couldn't do a month ago. Do this monthly. In a year, you'll have a beautiful record of growth — and you'll be surprised how much progress is invisible until you write it down.
