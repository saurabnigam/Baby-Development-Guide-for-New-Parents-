# Module 12: Age 5 to 7 — School Readiness & Friendships

> Time: 3 hours | Difficulty: Intermediate | Focus: early academics, friendship skills, self-control, confidence

---

## Key Takeaways

- School readiness depends on regulation, language, curiosity, and independence as much as on letters and numbers.
- Ages 5 to 7 are a major period for executive function, rule-following, and friendship skill-building.
- Children still need play, movement, sleep, and emotional coaching even when school begins.
- Parents should support competence without turning learning into pressure.
- Friendship skills must be taught directly: joining play, waiting, repairing, and handling disappointment.

---

## What Is Happening in the Brain

Children at this stage can hold rules in mind more consistently, follow classroom routines, and engage in longer stretches of focused activity. Working memory and inhibitory control are improving, though still immature. Emotionally, children are increasingly aware of peer approval and comparison.

This means school can strengthen confidence or trigger self-doubt depending on the environment and adult response. Children need support learning that effort, practice, and mistakes are normal.

---

## IQ Milestones: Age 5 to 7

Typical cognitive milestones include:

- Recognizes more letters, sounds, and simple words
- Understands basic number concepts and early operations
- Follows multi-step directions more reliably
- Understands simple rules, categories, and sequences
- Begins more logical thinking about concrete situations
- Improves attention span and task persistence

---

## EQ Milestones: Age 5 to 7

Typical social-emotional milestones include:

- Forms more stable friendships
- Understands rules and fairness more clearly
- Begins comparing self to peers
- Can recover from disappointment with support
- Uses more words and fewer meltdowns to express feelings
- Shows growing conscience and concern about doing the right thing

---

## Best Activities for Cognitive and Emotional Growth

### 1. Games With Rules

Card games, board games, Simon Says, Red Light Green Light.

Why it works:
- Builds inhibition and working memory
- Strengthens frustration tolerance
- Teaches flexibility and recovery after mistakes

### 2. Read-Aloud + Discussion

Ask:
- “Why did that happen?”
- “What might happen next?”
- “How do you think that character felt?”

Why it works:
- Builds comprehension and inference
- Strengthens empathy and perspective-taking
- Supports school success without drill-based pressure

### 3. Real-Life Math and Literacy

Count ingredients, read signs, sort laundry, write labels, make checklists.

Why it works:
- Makes academics meaningful
- Builds competence and confidence
- Supports transfer to daily life

### 4. Friendship Coaching

Practice phrases such as:
- “Can I join?”
- “I need space.”
- “Can we do one turn each?”
- “I didn’t like that.”

Why it works:
- Builds social navigation skills
- Supports boundaries and cooperation
- Reduces reactive conflict

### 5. Effort Reflection

At the end of the day ask:
- “What was hard today?”
- “What did you keep trying at?”

Why it works:
- Builds growth mindset
- Reduces perfectionism
- Strengthens resilience

## Recommended Toys & Materials: Age 5 to 7

Children are developing academic skills, sports competence, and rule-based strategic thinking. Materials should support growing executive function, creativity, and social learning across multiple sessions.

| Toy / Material | Why It Helps |
|----------------|-------------|
| Board and card games with real strategy — including beginner chess and draughts | Strategy, working memory, managing frustration and sportsmanship |
| Early chapter books and reader series | Reading fluency, independence, comprehension, empathy |
| Interlocking bricks or magnetic construction systems | Spatial reasoning, multi-step planning, creative engineering |
| Beginner science kits (chemistry sets, growing kits) | Hypothesis testing, curiosity, process thinking |
| Sports equipment (ball, bat, jump rope, hula hoop) | Motor skills, coordination, team and solo physical play |
| Art supplies (watercolor, modeling clay, collage) | Creativity, fine motor, emotional processing |
| Puzzle map or globe | Geography, spatial thinking, curiosity about the world |
| Musical instrument or beginner lessons | Attention, procedural memory, emotional development |
| Nature journal or collection kit | Systematic observation, classification, patience |

**Key principle:** This age benefits from activities that take multiple sessions to complete. Building or growing something over time builds patience and a sense of mastery that transfers to academic challenge.

## Nutrition Guidance for Age 5 to 7

### Priorities

- Steady breakfast with protein and fiber
- Regular meals to protect attention and mood
- Hydration and bowel regularity
- Balanced intake of iron, healthy fats, calcium, and protein

Children often appear inattentive or irritable when they are under-slept, hungry, constipated, or over-scheduled.

### Nutrition Milestones: Age 5 to 7

| Marker | Typical Range |
|--------|---------------|
| Energy | Approximately 1,400 to 1,800 kcal per day |
| Iron | 10mg/day; risk of insufficiency increases when meals are consistently low in iron-rich food |
| Calcium | 1,000mg/day; critical for continued bone mass accumulation |
| Vitamin D | 600 IU per day; supplement if dietary intake and sunlight are both low |
| Omega-3 fatty acids | Oily fish 1 to 2 times per week; walnuts; flaxseed |
| Protein | Approximately 19g/day |
| School lunch | Becomes a key daily nutrition source; involve children in choosing and packing |
| Breakfast composition | Protein and complex carbohydrate support sustained morning attention at school |
| Ultra-processed snacks and high-sugar cereals | Reduce intake; they displace higher-nutrient foods and blunt appetite |

Children who eat breakfast before school show measurably better attention, memory, and mood through the morning. Breakfast protein is particularly linked to sustained concentration.

## Sleep, Screens & Movement at School Age

The three things that most affect how a 5-to-7-year-old copes with school, and the three most likely to quietly deteriorate once school starts.

**Sleep: 9–12 hours in 24, and the wake time matters more than the bedtime.** School-age children who are short on sleep rarely look sleepy — they look inattentive, irritable, impulsive, or "hyperactive." If your child's behaviour deteriorated when school started, count backwards from the wake time before concluding anything else. Work out what time they need to sleep and set bedtime from there.

**Screens.** The shift at this age is from time limits to habits: no screens in the last hour before bed, devices charging outside the bedroom, no screens at meals, and outdoor play before entertainment media. See [Module 18](../18-screen-time-technology/README.md).

**Movement.** At least an hour of moderate-to-vigorous activity a day, and it is not a nice-to-have — physical activity in this age group is associated with better attention, mood, and academic outcomes. A child who has sat still all day needs to move *before* homework, not after.

**Water competence.** Ages 5–7 is when most children can achieve real water competence, and drowning risk shifts from "unsupervised access" to "overestimating ability." If your child hasn't had swim lessons, this is the window. See [Module 23, Part 3](../23-life-skills-critical-moments/README.md#part-3-water--swimming).

## Parent Behaviors That Matter Most

- **Connection before correction**, especially in the first 20 minutes after school — a decompression buffer with food, movement, and no questions works better than an interrogation
- Ask about **effort and difficulty**, not marks: "what was hard today?" beats "what did you get?"
- Let them experience the natural consequence of forgotten homework and forgotten kit — the teacher is a better teacher of this than you are
- Don't do it for them because it's faster. This is the year that habit sets for a decade
- Model mistakes out loud: "I got that wrong. Let me try again."
- Protect unstructured play. It doesn't stop being developmentally essential because school started
- Stay in contact with the teacher before there's a problem, not only after

## Common Challenges

### School Resistance

What helps:
- Predictable morning routine
- Calm validation plus steady follow-through
- Connection before correction
- Coordination with teachers if distress persists

### Friendship Hurt

What helps:
- Validate without overdramatizing
- Help the child tell the story clearly
- Practice repair and boundary language
- Watch for repeated exclusion or bullying patterns

### Perfectionism

What helps:
- Praise process, not only outcome
- Model mistakes openly
- Avoid over-correcting every small error

---

## Red Flags: Talk to a Professional If

- School adjustment is extremely difficult for a sustained period
- Emotional or behavioral distress is severe across home and school
- Social isolation is persistent
- Learning, attention, or language concerns are becoming more obvious

---

## Research Notes

- Executive function in early school years strongly predicts later academic performance.
- Read-aloud discussion supports literacy and emotional understanding together.
- Peer relationships in early elementary years influence self-esteem and school engagement.

---

## 5-Minute Action Plan

Add one daily question after school: “What felt easy today, and what felt hard?” This supports emotional processing and helps you spot emerging needs early.
