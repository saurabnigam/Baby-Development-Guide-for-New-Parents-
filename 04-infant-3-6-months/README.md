# Module 04: Infant - 3 to 6 Months

> Time: 3 hours | Difficulty: Beginner | Focus: social engagement, reaching, rolling, early communication, feeding readiness

---

## Medical Safety Note

This guide is for parent education, not a substitute for your paediatrician. Use your baby's corrected age if your baby was born early. Ask your paediatrician before changing feeding if your baby has prematurity, poor weight gain, reflux, eczema, allergy history, anemia risk, feeding difficulty, or any medical condition.

---

## The Short Version

If you read nothing else in this module:

- From 3 to about 6 months, breast milk or iron-fortified formula remains the baby's food. Do not start solids just to improve sleep.
- CDC, AAP, and WHO guidance centers complementary foods around about 6 months, when the baby shows readiness signs. Foods before 4 months are not recommended.
- Breastfed or partially breastfed babies need vitamin D supplementation unless your paediatrician gives different advice.
- Iron becomes more important in the second half of infancy. At the 4-month visit, ask whether your baby needs iron drops before solids begin.
- The 4-month and 6-month milestones should be used to observe and support development, not to drill the baby.
- Developmental neuroscience and behavioural research support responsive back-and-forth interaction, parentese, motor exploration, affectionate touch, sleep, and caregiver co-regulation as practical levers for brain development.
- No screen-based learning is needed. Babies learn from faces, voices, touch, movement, and safe exploration.

---

## Key Takeaways

- This is a social "serve and return" stage. Smiles, coos, squeals, eye contact, and imitation are brain-building events.
- Hands become a learning tool. Looking at hands, bringing hands to mouth, batting, reaching, grasping, and mouthing all support cognition.
- Tummy time changes from "exercise" into exploration: reaching, turning, rolling practice, and looking around.
- Emotional development grows through calm repetition: warm voice, predictable routines, repair after crying, and sensitive pauses when the baby turns away.
- Diet is simple until around 6 months: milk first, vitamin D if needed, iron discussion at checkups, and no pressure to start solids early.

## Start Here If Your Baby Is 2 to 3 Months Now

You are not behind. The best way to prepare for 3+ months is to make a small daily rhythm you can repeat.

### Keep Doing

- Feed on cues and track growth with your paediatrician.
- Do short tummy-time attempts while awake and supervised.
- Talk, sing, and respond when your baby coos or looks at you.
- Keep safe sleep consistent.
- Protect your own rest and regulation as much as possible.

### Start Adding Over the Next 2 to 4 Weeks

- Put one safe toy near the baby's hand during floor time.
- Pause during songs and wait for a sound, smile, or body movement.
- Offer safe objects to touch and mouth, such as a teether, cloth book, or soft rattle.
- Use a mirror for 1 to 3 minutes at a time.
- Narrate simple routines: feeding, nappy change, bath, getting dressed.

### Do Not Rush

- No flashcards, academic drills, baby videos, walkers, or sitting devices for "training."
- No water, juice, honey, cow's milk as a drink, salt, sugar, or solids before your baby is ready.
- No forcing tummy time until crying hard. Short and frequent works better.

## What Is Happening in the Brain

Between 3 and 6 months, your baby's brain is getting better at connecting what they see, hear, feel, and do. They begin to predict patterns: "When I smile, you smile." "When I kick, the toy moves." "When I fuss, someone helps me."

This is early IQ and EQ together:

- IQ grows through sensory exploration, repetition, cause and effect, attention, and motor planning.
- EQ grows through warm response, trust, shared joy, soothing, and learning that feelings can be organized with help.

At this age, development does not come from doing more. It comes from doing small things responsively, many times.

---

## Expert Lens: What Actually Builds the Brain

Think like a paediatrician, developmental neuroscientist, and behavioural scientist at the same time:

- The paediatrician asks: Is the baby safe, growing, feeding, sleeping, hearing, seeing, moving, and meeting broad milestones?
- The neuroscientist asks: What repeated experiences are shaping attention, sensory systems, motor planning, stress regulation, and social brain networks?
- The behavioural scientist asks: Which parent behaviours are easy to repeat every day and are actually changing the baby's learning environment?

The answer is not "more stimulation." The answer is better loops.

### The Core Loop

1. Baby gives a signal: look, sound, hand movement, smile, fuss, turn-away.
2. Adult notices it.
3. Adult responds warmly and specifically.
4. Adult pauses.
5. Baby gets a chance to respond again.

That small loop trains prediction, attention, communication, and emotional safety.

Example:

Baby looks at a rattle.

Parent: "You see the rattle."

Parent waits.

Baby kicks.

Parent: "Kick. You made it move."

Parent waits again.

This is a better brain-building activity than a noisy toy playing by itself.

### Evidence-to-Action Map

| Research idea | What it means in plain language | What to do daily |
|---------------|---------------------------------|------------------|
| Contingent response | Babies learn from responses that match their signal in timing and meaning | Copy a coo, answer a smile, respond to a reach, pause for the next turn |
| Parent-infant synchrony | Social timing helps babies organize attention, emotion, and arousal | Use short face-to-face play, songs, gentle rhythm, and warm eye contact |
| Parentese and turn-taking | Musical, slower, real speech helps babies attend and join conversation | Speak in real words with melody, then leave quiet space for baby to answer |
| Sensorimotor learning | Movement is cognition in infancy; hands, mouth, eyes, posture, and reaching build knowledge | Give safe floor time, tummy time, side-lying reach, and mouth-safe objects |
| Social brain specialization | Faces, voices, touch, and emotional expressions are high-value learning input | Use your face, voice, and gentle touch more than screens or electronic toys |
| Co-regulation | Babies borrow adult nervous-system calm before they can self-regulate | Reduce stimulation, hold, rock, feed, or soothe before expecting "independence" |
| Sleep consolidation | Sleep supports learning, memory, and regulation | Protect naps and bedtime rhythm without turning sleep into a battle |
| Nutrition as brain substrate | The brain needs energy, fat, iron, iodine, choline, DHA, protein, and vitamin D status | Keep milk primary, follow vitamin D guidance, ask about iron, prepare iron-rich solids around 6 months |

### What This Means for IQ

At 3 to 6 months, "IQ development" is not academics. It is:

- attention: looking longer, shifting gaze, tracking
- prediction: knowing what comes next in songs and routines
- cause and effect: kick makes sound, hand moves toy, cry brings help
- working memory beginnings: familiar face, familiar song, familiar sequence
- sensorimotor problem-solving: reach, grasp, mouth, turn, roll, try again
- language foundations: hearing real speech, turn-taking, sounds with social meaning

Parent behaviour that supports this:

- narrate what baby is already doing
- create one small challenge, not ten distractions
- wait before helping
- repeat the same game over days
- use daily care as learning time

### What This Means for EQ

At 3 to 6 months, "EQ development" is not teaching manners or independence. It is:

- felt safety: "My caregiver comes back and helps."
- emotional matching: "My smile, surprise, and discomfort are noticed."
- repair: "When I get upset, we can calm and reconnect."
- body regulation: feeding, burping, sleep, touch, rhythm, and reduced stimulation
- social confidence: "People are interesting and mostly safe."

Parent behaviour that supports this:

- respond before the baby is fully distressed when you can
- use a calm voice during fussing
- respect turn-away as a break cue
- reconnect after crying or overstimulation
- avoid making every interaction a performance test

### The Expert Rule: Match the State

The same activity can be helpful or too much depending on the baby's state.

| Baby state | What you see | Best parent move |
|------------|--------------|------------------|
| Quiet alert | eyes open, body relaxed, looking | talk, mirror, book, slow rattle tracking |
| Active alert | kicking, excited sounds, reaching | floor play, toy reach, song with actions |
| Overstimulated | turns away, stiffens, hiccups, fusses, frantic movement | reduce light/sound, hold still, slow voice |
| Tired | yawns, red eyes, gaze drifting, jerky movement | stop play, routine, sleep support |
| Hungry | rooting, hands to mouth, fussing, searching | feed first, play later |

Expert parenting here is not doing the "best" activity. It is choosing the activity that fits the baby's nervous system right now.

## Development Map: 3 to 6 Months

Use this as a guide, not a test.

| Age window | What often emerges | How to support it |
|------------|--------------------|-------------------|
| 3 to 4 months | steadier head, hand watching, cooing, social smiles, batting at toys | face-to-face play, tummy time, one reachable toy, slow talking |
| 4 to 5 months | stronger reaching, more laughter, hands to mouth, better tracking, interest in routines | rattle games, mirror play, song pauses, side-lying reach |
| 5 to 6 months | rolling practice, squeals/raspberries, turn-taking sounds, mouthing objects, early refusal cues | safe floor space, back-and-forth sounds, texture play, responsive feeding cues |
| Around 6 months | sits with support or briefly, reaches for wanted toys, explores by mouth, may show food readiness | prepare for solids only when readiness signs are present |

## CDC Milestones to Watch

### Around 4 Months

Many babies by 4 months:

- Smile to get attention.
- Chuckle when you try to make them laugh.
- Look, move, or make sounds to keep your attention.
- Coo and make sounds back when you talk.
- Turn toward your voice.
- Open their mouth when hungry and seeing breast or bottle.
- Look at their hands with interest.
- Hold their head steady when held.
- Hold a toy placed in their hand.
- Swing an arm at toys.
- Bring hands to mouth.
- Push up onto elbows or forearms during tummy time.

### Around 6 Months

Many babies by 6 months:

- Know familiar people.
- Like looking at themselves in a mirror.
- Laugh.
- Take turns making sounds with you.
- Blow raspberries or squeal.
- Put things in their mouth to explore.
- Reach to grab a wanted toy.
- Close lips to show they do not want more food.
- Roll from tummy to back.
- Push up with straight arms on tummy.

If your baby is not doing several expected skills, has lost skills, seems very floppy or stiff, does not respond to sound, does not visually track, rarely vocalizes, or you feel concerned, talk to your paediatrician early.

## Daily Rhythm That Actually Works

Do not force a rigid schedule. Use a repeated flow inside each awake window:

1. Feed and burp.
2. Nappy change or simple care routine with talking.
3. One connection activity.
4. One movement activity.
5. Quiet wind-down before sleep.

Most babies this age do best with short activities. Two minutes of connected play is useful. Ten minutes is plenty. Stop before your baby is overwhelmed.

### Example Day

| Time of day | Simple plan |
|-------------|-------------|
| Morning wake | Feed, nappy narration, 3 to 8 minutes tummy time, one song |
| Mid-morning | Feed, pram or balcony/window light, mirror play, nap |
| Afternoon | Feed, floor mat, reaching game, read 1 cloth book, nap |
| Late afternoon | Feed, walk around the house naming objects, texture play, nap if needed |
| Evening | Feed, bath or wipe-down, massage if baby likes it, dim lights, song, sleep routine |

This is only a model. If your baby feeds more often, naps unpredictably, or has short awake windows, keep the same order but shrink the activities.

## The 5 Daily Brain-Building Blocks

Aim for these across the day. They can happen during normal care.

### 1. Face-to-Face Talk

Goal: language, attention, social timing.

Do this:

- Hold baby 8 to 12 inches from your face.
- Say one short phrase.
- Pause for 3 to 5 seconds.
- Respond to any sound, smile, eyebrow lift, kick, or still attention.

Example:

Parent: "Good morning."

Baby: coos.

Parent: "You said good morning too."

Baby: kicks.

Parent: "Big kick. I saw that."

### 2. Tummy Time Plus Reach

Goal: neck, shoulders, rolling foundation, problem-solving.

Do this:

- Put baby on a firm floor mat while awake and supervised.
- Place one toy just within reach, slightly to the side.
- Let baby notice, try, rest, and try again.
- Encourage with your face and voice instead of instantly handing the toy over.

Start with 1 to 3 minutes if needed. Repeat several times daily.

### 3. Hands, Mouth, and Texture

Goal: sensory learning, hand-eye coordination, oral exploration.

Do this:

- Offer one safe item at a time: teether, cloth book, soft rattle, silicone spoon, clean textured cloth.
- Let baby hold, mouth, drop, and inspect.
- Name what is happening.

Example script:

"You found the ring. It is smooth. You put it in your mouth. Now it fell. I will give it back."

### 4. Song With Pauses

Goal: memory, rhythm, prediction, turn-taking.

Do this:

- Sing the same short song daily.
- Pause before the final word or sound.
- Wait for any response.

Example:

"Twinkle, twinkle, little..." pause.

If baby looks, smiles, kicks, or coos, answer: "Star."

### 5. Calm Repair

Goal: emotional safety and regulation.

Do this:

- When baby fusses, first reduce stimulation.
- Use a lower voice, slower movements, and one steady soothing method.
- After baby calms, reconnect briefly.

Example:

"That was too much. I am slowing down. I have you. We can try again later."

## How an Expert Would Watch a 10-Minute Play Session

You do not need special equipment. Watch four things.

### 1. Attention

Look for:

- Does baby look at your face, toy, sound, or light?
- Can baby follow something slowly?
- Does baby look away when tired?

Support:

- Hold one object steady instead of waving many things.
- Move slowly from center to left and right.
- Stop when baby repeatedly turns away.

Example:

"You looked at the red rattle. I will move it slowly. Now it is on this side."

### 2. Motor Planning

Look for:

- Does baby bring hands together?
- Does baby bring hands to mouth?
- Does baby swipe, bat, or reach?
- Does one side seem much stronger than the other?

Support:

- Use floor time instead of long container time.
- Place toys slightly to the side.
- Let baby try before moving the toy closer.
- Practice both left and right sides.

Example:

"Your hand is reaching. Almost. I will bring it a little closer. You touched it."

### 3. Social Communication

Look for:

- Does baby coo, squeal, smile, or move to keep your attention?
- Does baby respond differently to your voice than to random sounds?
- Does baby pause after you speak?

Support:

- Use real words with a warm, musical voice.
- Copy one baby sound, then add meaning.
- Leave a few seconds of silence for baby to answer.

Example:

Baby: "Aah."

Parent: "Aah. You are telling me. I am listening."

### 4. Regulation

Look for:

- Does baby calm with familiar voice, touch, feeding, rocking, or routine?
- Does baby get overwhelmed by too much noise, handling, or eye contact?
- Does baby recover after a short break?

Support:

- Reduce input first.
- Use one soothing strategy for long enough to work.
- After baby calms, reconnect gently.

Example:

"Break time. I am holding you. We are quiet now."

### The 10-Minute Expert Play Template

1. Minute 0 to 1: Check state. Fed? Awake? Comfortable?
2. Minute 1 to 3: Face-to-face talk with pauses.
3. Minute 3 to 6: Tummy or side-lying reach with one toy.
4. Minute 6 to 8: Texture or rattle exploration.
5. Minute 8 to 10: Slow song, cuddle, or quiet book.

If baby becomes tired at minute 3, stop at minute 3. That is not a failed session. That is responsive caregiving.

## Detailed Activity Menu

Pick 2 to 4 per day. Repetition is better than variety.

### 1. Mirror Conversation

Setup: Hold baby or place them supported near an unbreakable mirror.

Duration: 1 to 3 minutes.

Say:

- "There is your face."
- "You are looking."
- "I see your smile."
- "Mama/Papa is here."

What it builds:

- visual attention
- social awareness
- shared joy
- facial expression reading

Stop when:

- baby turns away repeatedly
- body stiffens
- fussing increases

### 2. Rattle Track and Grab

Setup: Use a lightweight rattle. Move it slowly from center to left and right.

How:

- Shake once.
- Pause.
- Move slowly.
- Let baby reach.
- If baby touches it, celebrate warmly.

Say:

"You heard it. You looked. You touched it."

What it builds:

- visual tracking
- sound-location connection
- reaching
- cause and effect

### 3. Side-Lying Reach

Setup: Roll baby gently onto their side on a mat. Place a toy in front of the upper hand.

How:

- Support with your hand behind the back if needed.
- Let the top hand reach forward.
- Switch sides after a short time.

What it builds:

- midline play
- rolling foundation
- hand coordination
- body awareness

### 4. Texture Trio

Setup: Choose three safe textures:

- soft cloth
- silicone teether
- crinkly fabric book

How:

- Offer one item at a time.
- Let baby explore for 30 to 60 seconds.
- Name the texture and action.

Say:

"Soft cloth. Crinkle sound. Hard ring."

What it builds:

- sensory comparison
- attention
- early categorization

Safety:

- No small parts.
- No strings, beads, plastic wrappers, sharp edges, or loose fibers.
- Supervise constantly.

### 5. Peekaboo Beginnings

Setup: Use your hands or a thin cloth. Keep it brief.

How:

- Cover your face for 1 second.
- Reveal with a smile.
- Repeat only if baby enjoys it.

Say:

"Where did I go? Here I am."

What it builds:

- prediction
- social delight
- attention
- early object permanence foundation

### 6. The One-Toy Problem

Setup: Put one safe toy near baby but not directly in the hand.

How:

- Wait.
- Let baby look, reach, swipe, miss, and try.
- Move the toy slightly closer if frustration rises.

Say:

"You are trying. It moved. Try again."

What it builds:

- persistence
- motor planning
- spatial awareness
- frustration tolerance with support

### 7. Read One Page Three Ways

Setup: Use one high-contrast or cloth book page.

How:

- First pass: name the picture.
- Second pass: make a sound.
- Third pass: connect it to baby.

Example:

"Dog. Woof. You are looking at the dog."

What it builds:

- language mapping
- visual focus
- memory through repetition

### 8. Walk and Notice

Setup: Hold baby safely and walk around the home or outside.

Say:

- "Light."
- "Fan."
- "Tree."
- "Cold water."
- "Soft towel."

What it builds:

- vocabulary exposure
- attention shifting
- sensory mapping
- calm curiosity

### 9. Nappy-Change Turn Taking

Setup: Use the same 3 or 4 phrases during nappy changes.

Say:

- "Legs up."
- "Wipe."
- "Clean nappy."
- "All done."

Pause after each phrase. Watch for eye contact, movement, or sound.

What it builds:

- routine prediction
- language rhythm
- cooperation foundation

### 10. Emotion Mirroring

Setup: Use during everyday emotion.

Examples:

- Baby smiles: "You are happy. I am smiling too."
- Baby startles: "That sound surprised you. I am here."
- Baby fusses: "You are tired. I will help you."
- Baby turns away: "You need a break."

What it builds:

- emotional attunement
- trust
- early self-regulation
- later feeling-language foundation

## EQ: How to Respond Moment by Moment

### When Baby Coos

Do:

- copy the sound once
- answer with a real word
- pause for a response

Example:

Baby: "Ahh."

Parent: "Ahh. Yes, I hear you. Tell me more."

### When Baby Looks Away

Do:

- pause
- lower your voice
- wait before restarting

Looking away is often self-regulation, not rejection.

### When Baby Cries

Use a simple check:

1. Hungry?
2. Wet/dirty nappy?
3. Tired?
4. Gas/burp?
5. Too hot/cold?
6. Too much stimulation?
7. Needs contact?
8. Illness or pain signs?

Then choose one soothing action and give it time:

- hold upright
- rock slowly
- feed if hungry
- swaddle only if baby is not showing rolling signs and your paediatrician says it is still safe
- dummy
- dim room
- white noise at a safe volume

### When Baby Is Frustrated Trying to Reach

Do:

- wait a little
- encourage
- make the task slightly easier
- let success happen

Example:

"You are working hard. I will move it closer. You got it."

This builds confidence better than rescuing immediately or letting frustration escalate too far.

## Diet and Feeding: 3 to 6 Months

### Main Rule

From 3 months until about 6 months, breast milk or iron-fortified infant formula is the baby's nutrition. Solids are practice and nutrition support later; they are not needed yet unless your paediatrician gives a specific medical plan.

### Breastfeeding

Most exclusively breastfed babies feed every 2 to 4 hours on average, but some cluster feed at certain times of day. Feed on cues rather than forcing a clock.

Common hunger cues:

- rooting
- hands to mouth
- lip smacking
- turning toward breast
- increased alertness or fussing

Common fullness cues:

- relaxed hands and body
- turning away
- slower sucking
- falling asleep content
- unlatching repeatedly

Ask for help if:

- wet nappies drop
- weight gain is a concern
- feeds are painful
- baby coughs/chokes often during feeds
- baby is very sleepy and hard to feed
- you feel feeding is not working

### Formula Feeding

Use iron-fortified infant formula unless your paediatrician recommends something else.

Practical expectations:

- Formula-fed babies often feed on a somewhat more regular pattern.
- By 6 months, many take about 6 to 8 ounces per feed at 4 or 5 feeds in 24 hours.
- Many paediatric sources advise that babies usually not average more than about 32 ounces formula per 24 hours unless the paediatrician is guiding it.
- Watch your baby's hunger and fullness cues rather than pushing the bottle to empty.

Responsive bottle-feeding tips:

- Hold baby upright or semi-upright.
- Keep the bottle more horizontal so baby can pace.
- Pause during feeding.
- Switch sides sometimes.
- Stop when baby turns away, loses interest, or relaxes.

### Vitamin D

Children younger than 12 months need 400 IU vitamin D daily.

- Breastfed babies need vitamin D drops starting shortly after birth.
- Partially breastfed babies usually need vitamin D drops too.
- Fully formula-fed babies may not need extra vitamin D if they drink at least about 27 ounces (800ml) of fortified formula per day.
- Ask your paediatrician if unsure.

### Iron

Iron supports brain development, attention, learning, growth, and immune function.

Ask at the 4-month checkup:

- Does my baby need iron drops before solids?
- Does prematurity or low birth weight change the plan?
- When should we start iron-rich foods?

Formula usually contains iron. Breastfed or mixed-fed babies may need individualized advice before 6 months. Around 6 months, iron-rich foods become a priority.

### What Not to Give Before 6 Months Unless Your Paediatrician Says So

- water
- juice
- cow's milk as a drink
- plant milks as a drink
- honey
- tea, coffee, sweet drinks
- salt or sugar
- adult snacks
- solids before readiness

### Parent Diet If Breastfeeding

You do not need a perfect diet to make good milk. Aim for steady nourishment.

Daily priorities:

- Protein at most meals: dal, beans, eggs, dairy, fish, chicken, tofu, paneer, meat, soy, nuts/seeds in safe adult forms.
- Iron-rich foods: lentils, beans, greens, eggs, meat, fish, fortified foods.
- Vitamin C with plant iron: citrus, amla, guava, berries, tomato, capsicum.
- Healthy fats: nuts/seeds, avocado, olive/mustard/groundnut oil, low-mercury fish if you eat fish.
- Iodine: iodized salt if used in your household.
- B12: especially important if vegetarian or vegan; discuss supplements with your clinician.
- Fluids: drink to thirst, especially around feeds.

Simple breastfeeding-parent meal examples:

- Moong dal khichdi with vegetables, curd, and fruit.
- Egg or paneer bhurji with roti and a vegetable.
- Oats cooked with milk, nuts/seeds powder for the adult, and fruit.
- Rice, dal, fish/chicken/tofu, cooked vegetables.
- Chana or rajma with rice, salad or cooked vegetables, and lemon.

## Preparing for Solids Around 6 Months

### Readiness Signs

Look for several signs together:

- sits alone or with support
- good head and neck control
- opens mouth when food is offered
- swallows food instead of pushing it all out
- brings objects to mouth
- tries to grasp small objects
- can move food from front to back of tongue

Interest in your food alone is not enough.

### First 2 Weeks of Solids: Simple Plan

Only start when your baby is about 6 months and ready, or when your paediatrician advises.

Keep milk first. Offer solids after a milk feed, once a day at first.

Day 1 to 3:

- 1 to 2 teaspoons smooth, thin puree.
- Try iron-rich first foods if possible.
- Stop when baby turns away, closes lips, cries, or loses interest.

Day 4 to 7:

- Continue once daily.
- Add one new single-ingredient food at a time.
- Keep texture smooth and soft.

Week 2:

- Continue small amounts.
- Begin offering a little variety.
- Keep iron-rich foods frequent.

### First Food Examples

Iron-rich options:

- iron-fortified oat, barley, multigrain, or rice cereal mixed with breast milk or formula
- smooth dal or lentil puree
- mashed beans thinned well
- tofu puree
- egg puree or very soft cooked egg mashed smooth
- chicken, fish, or meat puree if your family eats these

Other useful first foods:

- mashed sweet potato
- pumpkin
- carrot
- avocado
- banana
- pear
- unsweetened yogurt
- soft khichdi blended smooth
- ragi porridge made thin and smooth

Avoid:

- salt
- sugar
- honey
- whole nuts
- thick nut butter globs
- hard raw foods
- round choking hazards
- adult packaged snacks

### Allergen Introduction

When solids have started and your baby is tolerating basic foods, introduce common allergens in safe forms. Examples include egg, peanut, dairy, wheat, fish, soy, sesame, and tree nuts.

Safety notes:

- If your baby has severe eczema, known egg allergy, or another allergy concern, ask your paediatrician before peanut or other allergen introduction.
- Never give whole nuts.
- Thin smooth peanut butter with warm water, breast milk, formula, or puree until it is runny.
- Introduce one new allergen at a time, in the morning or earlier in the day, when you can observe.

## Sleep and Regulation: 3 to 6 Months

### Sleep Expectations

Sleep is still developing. Night waking can be normal.

Broad ranges:

- Around 3 months, many babies still need roughly 14 to 17 hours total sleep in 24 hours.
- From 4 to 12 months, many need about 12 to 16 hours total sleep in 24 hours, including naps.

Watch your baby more than the clock.

Tired signs:

- staring away
- red eyebrows or eyes
- yawning
- fussing
- jerky movements
- rubbing face
- losing interest

### Simple Sleep Routine

Use the same short sequence:

1. Dim lights.
2. Nappy.
3. Feed if hungry.
4. Burp.
5. Song or phrase.
6. Into safe sleep space.

Example phrase:

"It is sleep time. I love you. I am nearby."

### Safe Sleep Rules

For every sleep:

- Put baby on the back.
- Use a firm, flat, non-inclined sleep surface.
- Use a fitted sheet only.
- Keep pillows, blankets, bumpers, and soft toys out of the sleep area.
- Room-share without bed-sharing, ideally until at least 6 months.
- Avoid overheating and head covering.
- Stop swaddling when baby shows signs of trying to roll.

If your baby falls asleep in a car seat, swing, carrier, or adult arms, move them to a safe sleep surface as soon as practical.

## Safety at This Stage

### Rolling and Falls

Assume rolling can start any day.

- Keep one hand on baby on changing tables.
- Do not leave baby on a bed, couch, counter, or chair.
- Use the floor for most play.
- Make tummy time and rolling practice supervised.

### Choking and Mouthing

Everything goes to the mouth now.

Avoid:

- coins
- buttons
- beads
- small toy parts
- pen caps
- older siblings' small toys
- plastic bags/wrappers
- strings or cords
- food pieces before readiness

Use the toilet paper roll test as a rough screen: if an object can fit through a toilet paper roll, it is too small for unsupervised baby play.

### Containers and Positioning

Use baby seats, swings, and bouncers sparingly and only as intended.

Better default:

- floor mat
- tummy time
- side-lying play
- being held
- pram walk

Avoid walkers. They do not teach walking and can be dangerous.

## Parent Behaviors That Matter Most

- Respond warmly to sounds, expressions, and movement.
- Pause often so baby can "answer."
- Keep play short and stop before overwhelm.
- Use daily routines as learning time.
- Repeat favorite games many times.
- Keep screens away during direct interaction.
- Notice your baby's cues: hunger, fullness, fatigue, overstimulation, and need for connection.
- Choose connection over performance. Babies do not need entertainment all day.

## A Realistic Weekly Plan

### Week 13 to 14

Focus:

- more face-to-face cooing
- hands-to-mouth exploration
- short tummy time several times daily

Try:

- mirror play once per day
- one toy placed near hand
- same song with pauses

### Week 15 to 16

Focus:

- reaching and batting
- head control
- noticing routines

Try:

- rattle track and grab
- nappy-change phrases
- side-lying reach

Ask paediatrician:

- vitamin D plan
- iron plan
- feeding concerns
- vaccines and well-visit schedule for your location

### Week 17 to 20

Focus:

- rolling space
- longer tummy play if baby tolerates it
- turn-taking sounds

Try:

- one-toy problem
- texture trio
- read one page three ways

### Week 21 to 24

Focus:

- safe floor exploration
- early sitting with support
- food readiness observation

Try:

- practice sitting supported on your lap
- high chair practice without food if baby has head control and your paediatrician agrees
- prepare first-food plan for around 6 months

Do not:

- replace milk with solids
- start solids if baby lacks head control or cannot sit with support
- pressure baby to eat

## Troubleshooting

### "My Baby Hates Tummy Time"

Try:

- 30 to 60 seconds more often
- tummy on your chest
- towel roll under chest if advised
- face-to-face floor time
- tummy time after nappy change, not right after a full feed
- stop before hard crying

### "My Baby Is Distracted During Feeds"

Try:

- quieter room
- feeding before baby is very upset
- dimmer lights
- fewer people talking nearby
- burp breaks
- follow hunger and fullness cues

### "My Baby Wants to Be Held All the Time"

This is common. Social awareness is increasing.

Try:

- babywearing when safe
- short floor play after feeds
- predictable soothing routine
- alternating held time with floor time

### "My Baby Is Not Rolling Yet"

Rolling varies. Support the foundation:

- tummy time
- side-lying play
- reaching across midline
- floor time outside containers

Ask your paediatrician if movement seems very one-sided, stiff, floppy, or delayed with other concerns.

### "My Baby Watches Screens Calmly"

Calm does not mean beneficial. Under 18 months, screen media is not needed except video chatting. Use music, your voice, books, walks, or safe floor play instead.

## Red Flags: Talk to a Paediatrician

Talk with your paediatrician if:

- baby stops doing a skill they previously had
- baby does not smile or engage socially by the late end of this period
- baby rarely makes sounds
- baby does not turn toward voice
- baby does not track people or objects
- baby does not bring hands to mouth
- baby does not reach for objects by around 6 months
- baby seems very stiff, floppy, or strongly one-sided
- feeding is consistently difficult
- wet nappies decrease
- growth is a concern
- you suspect hearing or vision problems
- crying seems extreme, painful, or different from normal

## 5-Minute Action Plan

Today, do this once:

1. Put baby on a safe floor mat while awake.
2. Place one safe toy near one hand.
3. Get face-to-face.
4. Wait 10 seconds.
5. Narrate exactly what baby does.

Example:

"You are looking. Your hand moved. You touched it. That was hard work."

This one tiny loop builds attention, effort, motor planning, language, and emotional confidence.

## Research Translation Notes

Use these notes to understand why the guide recommends simple, repeated interaction instead of stimulation overload.

### Strongest Practical Takeaways

- Responsive interaction matters more than toy complexity. The research signal is strongest for contingent back-and-forth responses, language-rich routines, and warm caregiver sensitivity.
- Motor development is cognitive development. Reaching, rolling, mouthing, and posture changes give babies new information about objects, space, body control, and people.
- Social input is multisensory. Faces, voices, emotional tone, and touch work together; this is why live caregiver interaction is different from passive media.
- Repair matters. Infant research does not imply that parents must respond perfectly. Babies experience small mismatches all day; calm repair and reconnection are part of healthy emotional learning.
- Stress biology is real, but ordinary fussing is not toxic stress. The concern is persistent, severe, unsupported stress or neglect. Normal crying with caring response is part of development.
- Nutrition and sleep are not separate from IQ/EQ. Tired, hungry, iron-deficient, or dysregulated babies have less capacity for attention and social engagement.

### Evidence Strength

| Area | Evidence strength | Parent conclusion |
|------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Safe sleep, milk feeding, vitamin D, iron, solids timing | strong paediatric/public-health guidance | follow paediatric guidance and individual medical advice |
| Responsive caregiving and parent-child interaction | strong developmental evidence | notice signals, respond, pause, repeat |
| Parentese and conversational turns | growing experimental and intervention evidence | use warm real speech and back-and-forth pauses |
| Motor exploration and cognition | strong developmental theory and observational evidence | prioritize safe floor time over containers |
| Social touch and synchrony | growing behavioural/neuroscience evidence | use affectionate touch when baby likes it; avoid overstimulating handling |
| Specific "brain boosting" products | weak or unsupported | skip flashcards, videos, and overstimulating gadgets |
