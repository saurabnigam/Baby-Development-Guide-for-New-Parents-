## Why This Guide Exists

**In the first years of life, a child's brain forms new connections at a rate of roughly a million per second** (Harvard Center on the Developing Child).

What you provide in those years — attention, food, safety, and calm — genuinely shapes how that brain gets built.

You may have heard that "90% of brain development happens before age 5." That's a misreading. What reaches about 90% by age 5 is brain **size**.

Development is not size. The systems behind judgement and self-control keep maturing into the mid-20s.

So the early years matter enormously. **They are not a closing door.** If you're reading this with a seven-year-old, or a fourteen-year-old, you have not missed anything.

Most parents feel lost anyway. There's too much advice, most of it confident and some of it wrong.

This guide tries to cut through that. Including by telling you when a popular claim is weaker than it sounds — there's [a list of those](20-resources-faq/README.md#things-everyone-knows-that-arent-true).

## Core Principles

### 1. Serve & Return Interaction
The single most important thing you can do for your baby's brain development is **respond to their cues**. When a baby babbles and you respond, when they point and you name the object, when they cry and you comfort them — these "serve and return" interactions build neural architecture (National Scientific Council on the Developing Child, 2004).

### 2. The IQ-EQ Connection
Cognitive and emotional development are not separate tracks — they are deeply intertwined. A child who feels emotionally secure (EQ) is free to explore and learn (IQ). A child who can regulate emotions (EQ) can focus and problem-solve (IQ). This guide always addresses both together.

### 3. Every Child Is Unique
Milestones are ranges, not deadlines. A child who walks at 9 months is not "smarter" than one who walks at 14 months. This guide provides typical ranges backed by research while emphasizing that variation is normal and healthy.

### 4. Parents Matter Most
No toy, app, or program can replace a responsive, loving parent. You don't need expensive equipment — you need presence, patience, and play.

## Research Foundation

This guide synthesizes findings from:

- **Harvard Center on the Developing Child** — Brain architecture, toxic stress, serve & return
- **Jean Piaget** — Stages of cognitive development
- **Lev Vygotsky** — Zone of proximal development, scaffolding
- **John Bowlby & Mary Ainsworth** — Attachment theory
- **Erik Erikson** — Psychosocial stages of development
- **Daniel Goleman** — Emotional intelligence framework
- **AAP (American Academy of Pediatrics)** — Clinical guidelines
- **WHO** — Growth standards, nutrition, developmental milestones
- **Recent neuroimaging studies** — fMRI/EEG studies on infant brain development
- **The Lancet Child & Adolescent Health** — Global child development series
