# Module 08: Toddler — 18 to 24 Months

> Time: 3 hours | Difficulty: Beginner | Focus: Two-word phrases, big feelings, early self-regulation

---

## Key Takeaways

- The second year brings a major jump in language, memory, self-awareness, and emotional intensity.
- Tantrums are common because the emotional system is ahead of the regulation system.
- Naming feelings, offering simple choices, and holding clear boundaries help regulation develop.
- Play, movement, sleep, and nutrition strongly affect behavior at this age.
- Your job is not to eliminate all frustration; it is to help your toddler move through it safely.

---

## What Is Happening in the Brain

At 18 to 24 months, toddlers understand much more than they can explain. They have stronger desires, better memory, and growing independence, but the prefrontal systems needed for impulse control are still very immature. This mismatch is why toddlers can seem brilliant one minute and overwhelmed the next.

Language often surges in this period, especially when parents continue to talk, read, and respond to the child’s attempts to communicate. Emotional regulation is still almost entirely co-regulated.

---

## IQ Milestones: 18 to 24 Months

Typical cognitive milestones include:

- Uses 2-word combinations in many children by the end of this period
- Follows simple 2-step directions with support
- Sorts by simple features such as shape or color
- Engages in multi-step pretend play
- Completes simple puzzles and matching tasks
- Remembers locations, routines, and familiar sequences

### Why These Matter

This is the transition from single-symbol thinking toward combining ideas. “More juice” is more than speech. It reflects conceptual combination.

---

## EQ Milestones: 18 to 24 Months

Typical social-emotional milestones include:

- Shows strong preferences and ownership language such as “mine”
- Has tantrums or emotional outbursts when blocked or tired
- Begins identifying some emotions in self or others
- Shows early empathy in simple ways
- Engages mainly in parallel play with other children
- Tests limits to understand predictability and control

### Why These Matter

Limit-testing is often a search for structure, not a rejection of you. Toddlers feel safer when adults are calm, clear, and consistent.

## Best Activities for Cognitive and Emotional Growth

### 1. Feeling Words in Real Time

Name feelings simply and repeatedly.

Examples:
- “You are mad.”
- “You feel sad because we stopped.”
- “You’re excited to go outside.”

Why it works:
- Builds emotional vocabulary
- Organizes internal experience
- Lowers parental reactivity too

### 2. Simple Choice Practice

Offer two clear choices you can accept.

Examples:
- “Walk or be carried?”
- “Banana or yogurt?”

Why it works:
- Supports autonomy
- Prevents unnecessary power struggles
- Builds decision confidence

### 3. Matching and Sorting Play

Use socks, blocks, lids, or spoons for sorting.

Why it works:
- Strengthens categorization
- Builds attention and early executive function
- Works well with household objects

### 4. Pretend Sequences

Pretend to feed a doll, tuck it in, and say goodnight.

Why it works:
- Builds sequencing, memory, and symbolic thought
- Supports empathy through role play
- Helps process daily routines emotionally

### 5. Movement Breaks

Use music, jumping, climbing, pushing, pulling, and outdoor walking.

Why it works:
- Supports sensory regulation
- Lowers accumulated stress and agitation
- Improves sleep and mood

### 6. Repair After Tantrums

After calm returns, reconnect.

Say:
- “That was hard.”
- “You were really upset.”
- “We can try again.”

Why it works:
- Teaches that conflict does not end connection
- Supports resilience and trust
- Builds the pattern of recovery, not perfection

## Recommended Toys & Materials: 18 to 24 Months

Toddlers now initiate pretend sequences, love construction, explore art, and need movement. Materials should support imitation, creativity, and both large and fine motor engagement.

| Toy / Material | Why It Helps |
|----------------|-------------|
| Simple 3 to 6 piece knob puzzles | Fine motor, shape recognition, problem-solving |
| Pretend food and kitchen accessories | Symbolic play, language, imitation of routines |
| Baby doll with blanket and simple accessories | Nurturing play, empathy foundations, sequencing |
| Chunky crayons, washable paint, large paper | Self-expression, fine motor, mark exploration |
| Wooden unit blocks in varied sizes | Construction, spatial reasoning, cause-effect |
| Ride-on push toy or low balance bike | Gross motor, coordination, independence |
| Simple color or shape sorting board | Early categorization, visual discrimination |
| Ball and soft basket | Throwing, aiming, gross motor, turn-taking |
| Xylophone or simple drum | Rhythm, cause-effect, joyful expression |

**Key principle:** Pretend play expands fast in this stage. Provide household props alongside toys: an old phone, measuring spoons, bags, and scarves are often the most used items.

## Nutrition Guidance for 18 to 24 Months

### Feeding Priorities

- Offer 3 meals and 2 to 3 snacks at predictable times.
- Keep nutrient density high because toddler intake is often uneven.
- Continue prioritizing iron, healthy fats, protein, zinc, and fiber.

### Helpful Feeding Practices

- Sit together when possible
- Serve one familiar food with new food
- Avoid grazing all day, which can reduce appetite for meals
- Limit juice and highly processed snack foods

### Watch Behavior Links

Toddlers often look “behavioral” when they are actually:
- hungry
- tired
- constipated
- overstimulated
- under-moved
### Nutrition Milestones: 18 to 24 Months

| Marker | Typical Range |
|--------|---------------|
| Energy | Approximately 1,000 to 1,400 kcal per day |
| Iron | 7mg/day; selective eating at this age increases risk of insufficiency |
| Calcium | 700mg/day; approximately 2 dairy or fortified-alternative servings |
| Vitamin D | 600 IU per day; supplement if dietary intake is low |
| Protein | Approximately 13g/day; eggs, cheese, legumes, and meat all contribute |
| Fiber | Sufficient fiber from fruits, vegetables, and whole grains helps prevent common toddler constipation |
| Full textures | Most children manage full family textures by 18 months; some need modification longer |
| Picky eating peak | Often peaks in this window; repeated neutral exposure is the evidence-based response |
| Family meals | Eating together and modeling variety is more effective than any pressure tactic |

Constipation is especially common in this stage. Adequate fluids, movement, and fiber-rich foods prevent most cases. Persistent constipation warrants pediatrician input.

## Parent Behaviors That Matter Most

- Stay calm enough to lend your nervous system to your child
- Use short phrases during dysregulation
- Hold limits without lectures
- Build routines around sleep, meals, and transitions
- Notice and praise recovery, effort, and kindness

---

## Common Challenges

### Tantrums

What helps:
- Prevent with routine, snacks, sleep, and transition warnings
- Reduce words during peak distress
- Stay nearby if safe
- Do not give in to unsafe or essential limits, but do stay warm

### Hitting or Biting

Often related to frustration, sensory overload, imitation, or limited language.

What helps:
- Stop the action calmly
- State the limit briefly: “I won’t let you hit.”
- Teach alternative actions later: stomp feet, squeeze pillow, ask for help

### Picky Eating

Normal in toddlerhood.

What helps:
- Repeated exposure
- Family-style modeling
- No pressure, bribing, or chasing with food
- Keep portions small and refill if wanted

### Sleep Resistance

What helps:
- Predictable bedtime routine
- Regular wake time
- Active daytime movement
- Calm, boring bedtime boundaries

---

## Red Flags: Talk to a Pediatrician If

- No meaningful words or phrases by the late end of this stage
- Very limited eye contact or shared attention
- No imitation or pretend play
- Extreme, persistent feeding or sleep difficulty with developmental concern
- Loss of language, social engagement, or motor skills

---

## Research Notes

- Emotion coaching and parent co-regulation are associated with better later self-regulation and social competence.
- Sleep duration and consistency strongly affect toddler mood, learning, and attention.
- Responsive feeding supports better long-term eating patterns than controlling approaches.
- Pretend play supports language, symbolic thought, and social understanding.

---

## 5-Minute Action Plan

Pick one recurring hard moment, such as leaving the park or getting into the bath. Add two things: a short transition warning and a consistent feeling phrase. Example: “Two more minutes, then bath. You may feel mad when park time ends, and I will help you.” That reduces surprise and builds emotional predictability.
