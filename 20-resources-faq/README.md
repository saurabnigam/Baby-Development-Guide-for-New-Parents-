# Module 20: Resources, FAQ & What We Got Wrong

> **Time**: 2 hours | **Difficulty**: Beginner | **Focus**: where to look things up, straight answers to the common questions, and a list of popular claims that don't hold up
>
> ⚕️ Parent education, not medical advice. See the [Disclaimer](../DISCLAIMER.md).

---

## Key Takeaways

- You don't need more sources. You need a short list of trustworthy ones.
- **Several things "everyone knows" about child development are wrong.** There's a list below, with what the evidence actually says.
- When a source makes you feel panicked or guilty, that's usually a signal about the source.
- If you're worried, ask early. Waiting is the risk.

## Emergency & Crisis — Put These Somewhere Physical

| Situation | What to do |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Choking, not breathing, unresponsive, seizure, serious head injury** | Local emergency number, now |
| **Suspected poisoning, or a swallowed button/coin-cell battery** | Emergency services — **battery ingestion is a same-hour emergency** |
| **Severe allergic reaction** — swelling, wheezing, collapse | Emergency services |
| **Fever in a baby under 3 months** | Same-day medical assessment |
| **After any submersion, if there's coughing or breathing trouble** | Same-day medical assessment |
| **Child or teenager talking about suicide or self-harm** | Don't leave them alone. Remove access to means. Get help today. **[Full guidance](../14-age-12-18/README.md#if-you-are-worried-about-suicide-or-self-harm)** |
| **You're at the end of your rope with a crying baby** | Put them down safely and walk away. **Never shake a baby.** [Why and how](../03-newborn-0-3-months/README.md#read-this-first-when-the-crying-wont-stop) |

**Crisis lines:** US & Canada **988** · UK & Ireland **116 123** (Samaritans), or text SHOUT to **85258** · India **14416** (Tele-MANAS) · Australia **13 11 14** (Lifeline).

**Write your local numbers on the fridge.** Not just in a phone.

## Trusted Sources

A short list beats a long one. These are the places worth checking when you want an answer.

### General child health and development

| Source | What it's good for |
|--------|-------------------|
| [HealthyChildren.org](https://www.healthychildren.org/) (AAP) | The best all-round parent-facing source. Feeding, sleep, safety, behaviour, milestones |
| [CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early."](https://www.cdc.gov/ncbddd/actearly/index.html) | Milestone checklists — the revised, 75th-percentile ones |
| [Harvard Center on the Developing Child](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/) | The science, explained well. Brain architecture, serve and return, toxic stress |
| [NHS Start for Life](https://www.nhs.uk/start-for-life/) (UK) | Pregnancy to 5, plainly written |
| [Raising Children Network](https://raisingchildren.net.au/) (Australia) | Excellent, broad, and covers ages this guide compresses |
| [WHO — child health](https://www.who.int/health-topics/child-health) | International guidance, useful outside US/UK contexts |
| [Zero to Three](https://www.zerotothree.org/) | Specialist on 0–3, especially social-emotional development |

### When something specific is wrong

- **Your paediatrician, GP, health visitor, or practice nurse** — first stop, always
- **[IDEA Part C early intervention](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/early-learning-early-childhood/)** (US) — you can refer your own child, and the evaluation is free by law
- **Speech and language therapist** — for talking, understanding, or eating and swallowing
- **Occupational therapist** — for movement, coordination, sensory, and daily-living skills
- **Child psychologist or family therapist** — for behaviour, mood, anxiety, or family difficulty
- **Registered dietitian** — for feeding and nutrition concerns
- **[National Child Traumatic Stress Network](https://www.nctsn.org/)** — after a traumatic event, bereavement, or disaster

### Reading original research yourself

If you want to check a claim rather than take someone's word for it:

- **[Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/)** — free abstracts and often full text. This is what was used to verify every citation in this guide
- **[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/)** — same literature, US National Library of Medicine
- **[Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/)** — systematic reviews, the strongest form of evidence summary

**Reading tip:** check the *number of people* and whether it was randomised. A finding from 40 children is a suggestion; a finding from 40,000, replicated, is closer to a fact.

## Books

Read critically, and expect to disagree with parts. No book replaces professional help when something is genuinely wrong.

**Understanding children**
- *The Whole-Brain Child* — Siegel & Bryson
- *How to Talk So Little Kids Will Listen* — Faber & King. The most immediately practical book on this list
- *The Scientist in the Crib* — Gopnik, Meltzoff & Kuhl
- *Becoming Attached* — Robert Karen, on attachment research and its history

**Behaviour**
- *No-Drama Discipline* — Siegel & Bryson

**Feeding**
- *Child of Mine* — Ellyn Satter, the source of the Division of Responsibility used throughout this guide
- *Helping Your Child with Extreme Picky Eating* — Rowell & McGlothlin

> **A caveat on the sleep and parenting shelf generally.** Popular parenting books vary enormously in how much evidence sits behind them, and confident tone is not a proxy for evidence. Where this guide cites research, the link is there — check it. Where a book asserts something with no source, treat it as one experienced person's opinion, which is worth something but isn't the same thing.

## Things "Everyone Knows" That Aren't True

Every one of these appears constantly in parenting content. Several appeared in earlier versions of this guide.

| The claim | What the evidence says |
|-----------|----------------------|
| **"90% of brain development happens before age 5"** | Misreading. **Brain volume** reaches ~90% by 5. Development — myelination, pruning, judgement and self-control — continues into the mid-20s. [More](../01-brain-science-fundamentals/README.md#what-doesnt-hold-up) |
| **"EQ matters more than IQ"** | From a 1995 trade book, not the literature. Meta-analysis finds emotional skills add *on top of* cognitive ability rather than exceeding it. They predict different things. [More](../00-prerequisites/README.md#a-claim-this-guide-will-not-repeat) |
| **"The marshmallow test predicts your child's future"** | The effect is real but far smaller than reported, and largely attenuates after accounting for family background. Waiting also depends on whether a child has learned adults' promises hold. [More](../21-building-eq/README.md#first-an-honest-note-on-the-marshmallow-test) |
| **"Poor children hear 30 million fewer words"** | Failed to replicate. Rested on 42 families. The better-supported finding is about conversational **turns**, not word counts. [More](../22-building-iq/README.md) |
| **"Mozart makes babies smarter"** | Meta-analysis of ~40 studies, 3,000+ people: no support. Any effect came from pleasant arousal — any music you enjoy does the same. *Learning* an instrument is different, and does help |
| **"Left brain logical, right brain creative"** | 1,011 brains, 7,266 regions measured: individual regions specialise, but **nobody has a whole-brain side preference** |
| **"Growth mindset transforms achievement"** | Two meta-analyses (365,915 and 57,155 people): overall effects weak. A real signal for students who are academically at risk or low-income. [More](../12-age-5-7/README.md#praise-what-actually-holds-up) |
| **"Screen time is driving the teen mental health crisis"** | The most careful analysis (n = 355,358) found the association explains **at most 0.4%** of variation in wellbeing. The strong screen findings are about under-2s, sleep, and displacement instead. [More](../18-screen-time-technology/README.md#weak-total-hours-and-teenage-mental-health) |
| **"Learning styles" — visual, auditory, kinesthetic** | Repeatedly tested, repeatedly unsupported |
| **"Sleep training damages attachment"** | Followed up at age 6 across 326 children — no difference on any outcome, including cortisol-based stress regulation. Genuinely your choice. [More](../16-sleep-and-development/README.md#sleep-training-an-honest-section) |
| **"You can spoil a baby by holding them"** | Not in the first year. Responsive comfort builds security |
| **"Delay peanuts and eggs to prevent allergy"** | **Reversed.** Early introduction reduces allergy substantially. [The trial](../05-infant-6-9-months/README.md#allergens-the-advice-has-reversed) |
| **"Crawling is a milestone"** | Removed from CDC checklists. 4.3% of healthy children never crawl on hands and knees |
| **"Baby walkers help babies walk"** | They don't, and may delay it. ~230,000 US emergency visits over 25 years. Banned in Canada. [More](../06-infant-9-12-months/README.md#sit-in-baby-walkers-please-dont) |

**The pattern worth noticing:** the sticky claims are the dramatic ones. A finding that says *this one thing determines your child's future* travels much further than *it's complicated and the effect is modest*. Be suspicious of parenting advice that is very confident and very simple.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### "What's the single most important thing I can do?"

**Respond to your child.** Notice what they're doing, answer it, and come back after you've got it wrong.

That's it. Everything else in this guide is elaboration.

### "My child isn't hitting milestones on time. Should I panic?"

No — but check the right things. Ranges are far wider than charts suggest: walking alone spans 8 to 17½ months and both ends are normal.

**Look at the pattern, not the single item:** are they progressing compared to three months ago? Is understanding keeping up? Are there several concerns or just one?

**Two things always warrant a call, regardless:** losing a skill they had, and a worry that keeps returning. [How to raise it](../19-common-challenges/README.md#say-it-properly).

### "Does early reading mean my child is gifted?"

No. Early reading is a poor predictor of later reading, and being read *to* matters more than reading early.

### "IQ or EQ — which should I focus on?"

Wrong frame. They predict different things and each caps how much of the other gets used. A child who can't calm down can't think; a child who gives up never finds out what they could have solved.

**The practical answer:** emotional skills are more teachable, so that's where deliberate effort pays off most.

### "Are educational apps worth it?"

For under-2s, no — babies learn very little from screens. For older children, the honest question isn't whether the app has some value but **what it replaced.**

### "How much should I worry about what my child eats?"

Look at the week, not the meal. If they get an iron-rich food most days and are growing, energetic, and stooling normally, you're fine.

**Get help if:** growth falls off, variety is very restricted, or meals are genuinely distressing. [More](../15-nutrition-brain-development/README.md).

### "Can I spoil my baby?"

Not in the first year. Responding quickly is the mechanism, not a bad habit.

### "My child behaves at school and falls apart at home. What am I doing wrong?"

Nothing. They spent all day holding it together and home is the only place safe enough to let go. It's a compliment delivered in the most exhausting possible format. [What helps](../12-age-5-7/README.md#the-after-school-meltdown).

### "How do I know if it's a phase or a problem?"

Four questions: does it **persist** over months? Does it show up **across settings**? Is it **getting worse**? Is it **affecting daily life** — eating, sleeping, learning, friendships?

Two or more yes: ask someone. And a **loss of skills** skips all four questions.

### "I lose my temper more than I want to. Is my child damaged?"

Almost certainly not. What predicts a secure child isn't the absence of rupture — it's the presence of **repair**.

Say what you did, own it without a "but", say what you'll try instead, and reconnect. [How](../21-building-eq/README.md#part-5-repair--the-most-underrated-skill).

If it's frequent, escalating, or frightening you, that's worth support — not shame. [Here](../19-common-challenges/README.md#if-youre-frightened-of-your-own-anger).

### "Is it too late? My child is already 7 / 12 / 15."

No. Almost every developmental window is **sensitive**, not **critical** — earlier is easier, later is not too late. Language, empathy, reading, maths, self-control and relationships can all be built later.

Anyone telling you a window has closed is usually selling something.

## How This Guide Was Built

Worth knowing, so you can judge it.

**What was done:** every claim was checked against a primary source during writing. Where a source couldn't be verified, the claim was removed rather than kept — including several well-known figures. Where evidence is contested, the guide says so instead of picking the more compelling side.

**Four citations were caught pointing at completely unrelated papers** during checking — a study on peritoneal dialysis, one on neonatal glucose monitors, one on bacterial sigma factors, and one on brachial plexus injury. All would have looked entirely plausible in a reference list. This is why every reference here carries a link: so you can check.

**What's still incomplete** is tracked openly in [TRACKER.md](../TRACKER.md), and the original audit that started this work is in [RED-TEAM-REVIEW.md](../RED-TEAM-REVIEW.md). Known gaps include the specific milligram-per-day nutrient figures, which differ between countries and could not be verified against a primary source.

**If you find something wrong here, it should be corrected.** Health content that doesn't get corrected gets worse over time.

## A Last Word

You do not have to optimise childhood. Children grow well in relationships that are responsive, reasonably structured, playful, and safe enough.

**"Safe enough" is doing a lot of work in that sentence, and it is deliberate.** The research does not describe perfect parents. It describes ordinary ones who notice, respond, get it wrong, and come back.

Your presence matters more than your performance.

## 5-Minute Action Plan

**Write down three things, on paper, and put them where you'll see them:**

1. Your doctor's number
2. One local crisis number
3. **The name of one person you could ring and ask for practical help** — someone who'd take the children for two hours

That third one is the one people skip, and it's the one that matters most on a bad day.

Support systems are a developmental input too. Yours counts.
